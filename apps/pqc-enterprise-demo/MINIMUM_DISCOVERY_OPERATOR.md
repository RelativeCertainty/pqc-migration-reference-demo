# Minimum effective PQC discovery — candidate operator guide

## What this candidate does

The contributor answers five questions about known products, helpful teams,
existing records, scope and restrictions. A referral, useful partial answer or
explicit unknown can finish that person's task. The assessment team then owns
investigation, evidence review and report preparation.

This is a synthetic, host-only development candidate. It is not enterprise SSO,
an adopted enterprise standard, a live connector or a migration tool. Never enter
an enterprise password, private key, customer record or other restricted data.

## Respond to a request

1. Open the exact request link supplied with the candidate handoff on this Linux
   host. Sign in with its assigned synthetic contributor identity and the
   displayed demo-only password. The link opens the request directly.
2. Read the request title and software examples. Examples identify a software
   class; they do not claim that a product is installed or approved.
3. Check the prominent **Software class** heading. In question 1, use **Add
   software or deployment** and **Add another software or deployment** to list
   several products in that class. A product name alone is enough; deployment
   and environment details are optional. Use separate entries for different
   deployments of the same product. You may instead name several products in
   the answer box, give a referral, or choose "I do not know" or "Not my team".
   There is no separate questionnaire for each product.
4. Select **Save draft**. The saved confirmation is the indication that the
   server retained the answer. Return through the same link to resume.
5. Select **Review and submit**, check your saved answers, then submit the
   response to the coordinator. The receipt explicitly says no further response
   is required now. It does not say that the source or assessment is complete.

No API pagination, six-person ownership roster, new policy document or promised
resolution date is required from the contributor.

## Recognize the software class

The web request names the **PQC Discovery Domain** and **Software class** before
the questions. **Products you may recognize** shows the full selected-class
list, with offering labels and official product links. Expand the other classes
in that domain, or **Browse all 10 PQC discovery domains**, without changing
your assignment or answers.

The separately versioned recognition reference contains 242 examples across
27 software classes: 16 for enterprise context, 25 for PKI and trust, 26 for
encrypted traffic, 20 for machine access, 26 for software delivery, 38 for
cloud/keys/identity, 27 for protected data, 29 for distributed endpoints, 17 for
specialized/regulated cryptography, and 18 for governance/assurance.

These are recognition examples, not a complete market inventory, a purchasing
recommendation, a confirmed enterprise stack, PQC-readiness proof or qualified
connector list. COTS means commercial off-the-shelf; OTS means off-the-shelf;
SaaS means software as a service. Open-source and relevant hardware/platform
examples are labelled too. Offering labels are broad; exact edition, version
and deployment require confirmation. A missing product is not excluded from
discovery. Several products or deployments may fulfill the same function.

The reference does not rewrite the five questions or their original examples.
Earlier saved question examples remain available separately. Browsing a product
never adds it as a discovered asset, answer, evidence record or approval.

## Choose the display theme

After signing in, use **Theme** in the questionnaire header or workspace sidebar
to choose **Dark** or **Light**.
The questionnaire and coordinator workspace use that same preference; changing
it does not save, submit, reset or otherwise change your response. The preference
is remembered by this browser when local preference storage is available. The
default is Dark, matching the technical workspace. The login design and fixed
printable report pages retain their intentional presentation.

This reversible presentation change is R1. Check labels, response controls,
optional help, warnings and keyboard focus in both themes through normal use.
Technical checks do not record an owner verdict or establish WCAG conformance.

## Ask for existing information, not proof

The initial request helps locate products, teams and existing information. It
does not ask the respondent to demonstrate compliance, create documents,
establish policy or resolve a restriction. A product name, document reference,
referral or "not sure" is useful. Do not submit credentials or sensitive files.
Any later collection or system access must be agreed separately with the
appropriate owner.

New requests use the versioned `pqc.discovery.v2` wording. The fifth question
asks about access restrictions, information-sharing considerations and known
gaps. Previously saved requests retain their original questions and answers;
the interface explains their older terminology. The same version-pinned
questions appear in the Excel counterpart. Existing exports, attributed
responses, submissions and reports are not rewritten.

Within technical review and reports, **evidence** means information supporting
a conclusion, with its source, date, scope and limitations recorded. Identifying
a reference is not the same as obtaining permission to examine it, examining it,
or verifying a technical claim. Those distinctions and their controls remain.

The wording-only change is R1: review the request, its optional help, Excel
instructions and submission receipt through normal use. This does not waive
the R2/R3 observations owed for the underlying workflow and authorization.
The legacy intake form also has a targeted draft-retention safeguard (R2): its
initial synchronization must not clear a first edit. Verify immediate entry,
save and readback separately from the wording review. No owner outcome is
inferred from automated tests.

## Work offline in Excel

### Open the complete collection

On **Discovery**, use **All 27 software-class forms, together**. It presents
the five core questions once, followed by a browseable catalog of all 27 classes
in the ten PQC Discovery Domains. Browsing does not create assignments. These
are the new short forms, not the older detailed specialist questionnaires.

As assessment lead:

1. Choose the intended synthetic **Collection recipient** and select **Review
   collection creation**. Confirm the assessment and recipient, then select
   **Create the 27 draft web forms**. This creates one new draft per class in a
   single transaction. It does not copy or change existing answers, send email,
   submit responses, or establish source readiness. One collection per recipient
   is permitted in this bounded candidate.
2. Under the saved collection, select **Prepare complete Excel collection**.
   This records an immutable snapshot of all 27 individually returnable forms
   and a consolidated reference workbook. Preparation is separate from download.
3. Select **Download all 27 Excel forms + consolidated guide (ZIP)**. Extract
   the ZIP; it contains `00_Consolidated_Questions.xlsx` plus ten domain folders
   containing 27 class-named Excel files. Individual download links are also
   available if an approved browser route does not permit ZIP downloads.
4. Read **Start here** in the consolidated workbook for the file/request index.
   **All questions** contains 135 question instances: the same five questions
   repeated across 27 classes, not 135 different questions. **Software examples**
   contains the complete 242-entry recognition reference. The consolidated
   workbook is read-only reference material, not an answer-return format.
5. Use **Open individual web forms and response status** to open the appropriate
   assigned request. Return its individual Excel file there using the normal
   preview, conflict resolution and apply-as-draft steps. Review and submit
   separately. Do not upload the ZIP or consolidated overview as a response.

Contributors see only their own collections and assignments. They do not create
collections or export packages for others. Having all forms available does not
mean one person must answer them all. Partial responses, referrals and unknowns
remain useful. Multiple products and deployments can be named within a class's
response; no extra questionnaire is required for each product.

Web edits do not alter an older downloaded package. Prepare a new snapshot when
needed, up to three retained packages per collection and the existing bounded
assessment/per-request export limits. A submitted request may appear in an
archival package, but remains submitted: returning it cannot bypass the explicit
reopen step. Old forms, submissions, exports and immutable assessment history
remain intact. Ordinary downloads do not authorize access or evidence admission.

Collection behavior is R2; recipient-specific collection access is an R3
checkpoint. Owner walkthrough is still required for the affected behavior:
recognize the five questions and 27 classes, create a collection, download it,
resume one form, reconcile an offline change, and verify another contributor
cannot see its private collection. Automated checks and synthetic test responses
are not owner outcomes or evidence of enterprise acceptance. This is a loopback
development candidate, not a production or public-ready release.

### Return one form

Open **Use the same five questions in Excel** after saving web edits. Prepare
and download the workbook. Its five definitions are the same version pinned to
the web request. Return the file through the same request, preview the proposed
changes and resolve competing whole-question edits before applying a draft.

Newly prepared workbooks show the selected domain's software examples on the
first **Discovery form** sheet. The **Software examples** sheet contains the
complete ten-domain reference, product types, recognition notes and official
source URLs as text. This third sheet is reference material, not extra homework;
editing it does not supply or change an answer. Keep its header and structure.
Use the blue answer cells on the first sheet for responses.

Already downloaded files cannot acquire these examples automatically. Prepare
a new Excel form to receive the reference. Keep any existing offline edits:
earlier two-sheet discovery workbooks still import through the same request,
and their stored export bytes and question definitions remain unchanged.
The detailed specialist questionnaires also remain unchanged.

Preparing a workbook does not confirm that the browser downloaded it. If Chrome
reports **Blocked by your organization**, retain the exact message for diagnosis;
that message alone does not identify the blocking policy or component. Do not disable protections, use
an alternate transfer route to evade the policy, or make the export public. You
can continue answering in the web form without downloading a file. Keep the
exact error message for the administrator; a browser download denial is not an
Excel file-corruption diagnosis. Chrome describes its managed download controls
in its [administrator download-restriction guidance](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/7579271?hl=en).

Blank Excel cells retain the export value; use the web form to clear a saved
answer explicitly. Importing does not submit a response. Workbook author text
is unverified attribution, not an authenticated approval or access grant.

The original detailed questionnaires and their exports remain under specialist
questionnaires. New five-question workbooks are a distinct format; they do not
replace or rewrite old 27-question or earlier intake files.

Recognition browsing is an R1 presentation change. The revised offline format
and old-file compatibility are an R2 checkpoint: the owner should prepare a
new form, recognize its examples, and verify an old edited form still previews
correctly before relying on the revised workflow. Automated checks do not
record that owner outcome. The candidate remains a development demonstration.

## Follow through as the assessment team

Use **Discovery** with the synthetic assessment-lead identity. Respondents do
not need this screen, an assessment-creation step or a change of persona.

1. Select a returned request and create an engineering investigation explaining
   the conclusion it needs to support.
2. Assign the investigation and add products or deployments identified during
   research. Staff can do this after a partial answer without reopening or
   rewriting the contributor's submission. Keep evidence-producing sources
   distinct from the systems they describe.
3. Record official documentation, a proposed investigation method and known
   limitations. Documentation research does not establish installed-product
   compatibility or permission to collect information.
4. Review the three draft standards packages: assessment/evidence, crypto
   agility and migration assurance. Record observed practice, reported existing
   requirements, possible conflicts and the proposed review authority separately.
   Use **Revise draft proposal** to edit the proposed text. Saving records a new
   revision; it does not adopt a company standard or rewrite an earlier report.
5. Controlled evidence staging requires the existing G00 and P1-G01 boundaries.
   The new evidence path accepts only the bounded synthetic TLS reference
   format, not arbitrary vendor exports. Other families can be researched but
   are not qualified by this one parser.
6. A different technical-reviewer identity records a factual qualification for
   the staged evidence bundle. The assessment lead can then admit qualified
   evidence. These are simulated development decisions, not owner validation
   outcomes or enterprise approval.
7. Open the assessment's map, assets and reports. Admitted TLS observations
   contribute actual endpoint records and separate key-establishment and
   certificate-signature uses. Staged records and questionnaire testimony do not.
8. Review the evidence package, then generate the Phase 1 report. A qualified
   Phase 1 handoff explicitly identifies the package Phase 2 may rely on. Phase 2
   adds the reviewed scenario, business consequence, uncertainty and next action;
   it does not pick a newer Phase 1 report automatically.

## How to read the reports

The discovery section separates submitted testimony, engineering work, admitted
evidence and proposed standards. A hybrid key-exchange setting does not erase a
classical certificate-signature dependency. Missing population, service
relationships, information lifetime and independent runtime verification remain
limitations. Proposed standards support recommendations, not retrospective
noncompliance findings.

Report bytes and their input fingerprints are immutable. Later answers,
standards revisions or evidence generate new review work; they do not rewrite
historical reports. Merely creating an empty request or exporting a workbook is
not technical progress.

## Boundaries and validation still owed

- Ten estate areas and 27 runtime source families are coverage classifications,
  not 27 compulsory assignments. The separate 27-template specialist catalog
  retains its own IDs and crosswalk.
- Only synthetic TLS evidence admission is exercised in this increment. No live
  ServiceNow, F5, GitHub, Vault or Azure integration is enabled.
- Findings are illustrative, evidence-linked interpretations, not approved risk
  scores or compliance determinations.
- Questionnaire and evidence files are bounded; unexpected active content is
  rejected. Synthetic state remains in the candidate's owner-only directory.
- Workflow changes are R2; authorization/evidence-admission changes are R3.
  Automated checks and our walkthrough are engineering evidence only. The owner
  must personally record the targeted usability and authorization observations
  before activation/readiness claims.
- Enterprise authentication, SQL Server qualification, external communication,
  formal standards adoption and Phase 3/4 execution remain separate work.

The candidate launcher binds only loopback, allows one CPU and 1 GiB memory,
disallows additional swap, and stops after two hours. It is not an always-on
deployment. Preserve the matching application release and state backup together
for rollback; never point an older release at new writable state.

## Research basis

The five-question set is a design choice to validate with users, not a NIST
minimum. Its division of responsibility follows the principle of asking only
questions with a clear purpose and suitable respondent in the
[GOV.UK question-protocol guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure).

The standards proposals draw on NIST risk-assessment and crypto-agility guidance,
including [SP 800-30 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) and
[CSWP 39upd1, final guidance](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/39/upd1/considerations-for-achieving-crypto-agility/final).
[IR 8547](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/ipd) remains an Initial Public Draft,
not a binding company requirement. The bounded hybrid-group interpretation uses
the [OpenSSL 3.5 group documentation](https://docs.openssl.org/3.5/man3/SSL_CTX_set1_curves/).
These references establish neither NIST certification nor enterprise adoption.
